Getting listed

Four steps, and the first three are free

Vendors do not buy credits to be found. Credits are spent by whoever creates and runs an event.

1

Build the listing

Category, city, the areas you cover, your price basis — per guest, per package or per day — and a real portfolio. The listing is what clients compare you on, so it is worth an hour rather than ten minutes.

2

Get verified

Government ID, business registration and proof of address. Nothing goes live and no booking can reach you until all three clear. This is the step that makes a client stop asking for references.

3

Receive briefs you can price

A brief arrives with the date, city, headcount and budget range already attached, and only if it matches your category, your area and your availability. Reply with a quote, or decline without it counting against you.

4

Deliver, and get paid out

The client pays the deposit in-app to hold the date and the balance before the event. Funds are released to you once delivery is confirmed — not whenever an invoice eventually surfaces.

Verification

Three checks, and all three are required

A badge that some vendors have and some do not is a badge nobody reads. Every listed vendor has cleared all three, with no partial state in between.

Identity

Government-issued ID matched to the person who controls the account. A business cannot be transferred to a different operator without re-verifying.

Business

CAC registration checked against the trading name on the listing, so the business a client books is the business that exists on paper.

Address

Proof of the operating address, which is also what lets the platform filter briefs by the areas you genuinely cover rather than the ones you would like to.

Ratings come only from clients who booked and paid through the platform, so a review cannot be bought or left by someone who never hired you.
