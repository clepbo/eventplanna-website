What is free, and what a credit is for

There is no subscription and no card kept on file. You buy credits once and spend them on four actions.

Always free

The workspace

Six things that stop falling through the cracks

Winning the brief is the visible part of the job. Everything after it is where events quietly go wrong, and where a shared workspace earns its place.

Budgets that reconcile

Allocate by category, land every quote, deposit and balance against a line, and watch allocated, spent and remaining stay in agreement. An alert fires at 85%, not after the fact.

Phases and tasks

Each event breaks into phases with their own checklists. Tasks carry an owner, a due date and a status, so the progress bar counts finished work instead of guessing.

Bookings end to end

Request, quote, accept, contract, deposit, balance, completion. Six states, each timestamped, so nothing important was agreed in a chat you cannot find in December.

A team with real roles

Managers, coordinators and assistants each get the access their job needs and see only the events they are assigned to. Adding someone costs 2 credits, once.

A client portal

Share progress, the budget and decisions that need a yes through a link, without handing the client the keys to your workspace or your cost base.

Messaging in context

Every thread sits against the booking it belongs to, so the quote, the revision and what was finally agreed stay together and survive a change of coordinator.

Your team

Three roles, and nobody sees more than they should

A coordinator running the day does not need your margins, and an assistant chasing a florist does not need the whole client list.

Manager

Full access to the events they own: budget, costs, bookings, team and the client portal. The only role that can change an allocation or accept a quote.

Coordinator

Runs delivery on the events they are assigned to, covering tasks, timeline and supplier contact, without budget figures or supplier pricing.

Assistant

Works the task list on a single event. Can complete, comment and upload, and sees nothing about money, other events or other clients.

The client portal

What the client sees, and what stays yours

Being transparent with a client should not mean handing over your cost base. The portal is a deliberate slice of the workspace, not a login to it.

They see

Progress against phases, the budget as you have chosen to present it, confirmed bookings, decisions waiting on them, and the documents you shared. Enough that they stop calling for updates.

They never see

Your supplier rates, your margin, internal notes, other clients, your team’s task chatter, or any event you have not shared with them. There is no permission that opens it.

Exporting a client-ready budget or progress pack costs 1 credit, and records the figures exactly as they stood at that moment.
