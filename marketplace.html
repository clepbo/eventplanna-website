Nobody plans an event badly on purpose. It goes wrong because the price, the plan and the people live in different places. One budget everyone is reading from fixes most of it.

Getting started

Four steps, and none of them is a phone call

Creating the event costs 5 credits, and every account starts with 200. Browsing the marketplace and messaging a vendor are free.

1

Describe the event once

Type, date, city, headcount and budget range. EventPlanna turns that into a workspace with phases, a starter checklist and a budget already broken into categories — so you are editing a plan rather than staring at a blank page.

2

Compare people who are actually free

Planners and vendors are filtered by your date, your city and your budget before you see them. Each profile carries a real portfolio, a price range, past events and a rating from clients who booked through the platform.

3

Send a brief, get a real quote

A booking request carries your date, headcount and budget with it, so the reply is a priced quote rather than “what is your budget?”. Accept one, pay the deposit in-app, and the date is held.

4

Follow it to the day

Every quote, deposit and balance lands against a budget line. Tasks carry an owner and a due date. If you hired a planner, you see the same screen they do — so “where are we?” stops being a phone call.

The money

A budget that reconciles itself

Allocated, spent and remaining are the only three numbers, and they are the same three numbers on everyone’s screen. Every deposit you pay in-app is attributed the moment it moves, so the figure you are looking at is current rather than last week’s.

  • Category allocations that add up to your total, or say plainly that they do not
  • Deposit and balance both tracked against the same booking
  • An alert when a category passes 85% of its allocation
  • A client-ready export for anyone else contributing to the cost
